## Introduction: The "Pristine Chinese" We Take for Granted Is Sedimentary Rock Laid Down After Five Great Shocks

A modernly educated Chinese writer picks up the pen to write a routine work retrospective:

> "In light of the serious problems that have come to light during the implementation of this project, we must carry out a deep reflection so as to ensure, in earnest, that every measure is thoroughly put into effect in the next phase, and that the team's execution capacity is fully activated."

In this brief passage of a few dozen characters, nearly every word and every grammatical marker conceals the layered geological folds of loanword erosion across centuries or even two thousand years:

- "Project," "problem," and "reflection" come from **Japanese-made Chinese** (washō kan'i) renderings of Western concepts during the Meiji Restoration;
- "Push forward," "put into effect," and "ensure" bear the unmistakable imprint of a **Soviet-origin and Yan'an-era political register**;
- The attributive-clause structure of "every measure" and the passive marker in "fully activated" are genetic legacies left by **the direct translation of Western European grammar**, typified by the Gospel of Matthew.

We habitually treat the over-literary Europeanized sentence, "correct nonsense," "AI-speak," or "translationese" as the personal bad habits of contemporary writers. But stretch the lens out to two thousand years and you will find: **written Chinese was never a pure, sealed, static specimen — it is an evolutionary history of being continuously shattered, restructured, and hybridized by foreign languages, and of that hybridization settling, in the end, into modern everyday usage.**

To make clear exactly where modern Chinese came from and where it has been carried to, we built a field-tested corpus of 242 core documents totaling 3.2MB (covering the *Analerta*, the complete 1919 Union Version of the New Testament, Mao Zedong's early and founding-era works, contemporary government work reports, and generated text across the five major LLMs), combining quantitative field testing with documentary research, in order to answer three questions:

1. Across two thousand years of foreign-language impact on Chinese, how many waves were there exactly? Which neural ganglion of Chinese did each wave strike?
2. What did the "vernacular grammar" that began, in the modern era, with the Bible Union Version actually do? Did it bring Europeanization, or did it open the road to de-classicalization?
3. Who exactly governs today's screens with "party-dogma eight-legged prose of the system" and "AI translationese"? And how should a modern writer detoxify their own writing?

### 1. Buddhist Scriptures Enter China: The Reconstruction of Nouns and a Causal Universe

The translation campaigns from the Eastern Han through the Tang–Song were the first cross-civilizational encounter that Chinese ever faced. The great translator-monks — Zhi Fahu, Kumarajiva (Jiǔluó Shī), Xuanzang, and others — confronted Sanskrit, a precise inflectional language. Buddhism's most core transformation of Chinese was **the expansion of its lexical and conceptual universe**.

Xuanzang codified the principle of "the five categories that are not translated," but the more far-reaching influence lay in borrowing existing Chinese characters to localize Buddhist philosophical concepts. According to Liang Qichao's statistics in *Translated Literature and the Buddhist Canon*, the Buddhist scriptures contributed tens of thousands of new words to Chinese. Today we blurt out off the top of our heads — "world," "cause and effect," "the real," "relative," "equal," "attachment," "vexation," "speaking from one's own experience" — and every one of them descends from the Buddhist canon.

At the same time, the **Sanskrit chant and verse** (four-, five-, and seven-syllable rhymed lines) brought in by the translations of the scriptures not only greatly stimulated the development of paired-parallel prose (*pìlèi wén*) of the Han–Wei and Six Dynasties, but also gave Chinese an unusually high tolerance for the "four-syllable rhythm."

### 2. The Reflux of Japanese-Made Chinese: Modern Academic Re-export Using Japan as a Stepping Stone

After the defeat in the Jiawu War of 1894–95, Chinese intellectuals launched a wave of study in Japan. By then Japan had already gone through several decades of the Meiji Restoration, and its Enlightenment-era scholars — Fukuzawa Yukichi, Nishi Shūshū, and others — had translated large bodies of Western philosophy and science using classical Chinese characters.

In 1900, Liang Qichao published, in Japan, a stone-printed *Method for Reading Japanese Texts in Chinese Characters*, opening up the remarkable sight of "Chinese literati directly reading Japanese books written in kanji." China did not transplant Latin roots directly; it accomplished a low-friction re-export through "Japanese-made Chinese":

- **Political institutions**: constitution, democracy, government, political party, republic, cadre, privilege;
- **Society and economics**: society, economics, finance, capital, investment, enterprise, consumption;
- **Natural sciences and the humanities**: physics, chemistry, biology, art, philosophy, psychology, logic.

The crafty thing about Japanese-made Chinese is that **its word forms look archaic and elegant, while the concepts are entirely new**. Chinese intellectuals, without knowing it, swapped out their underlying thinking models, and the cognitive framework of the modern disciplines was thereby fixed in place.

### 3. The Bible Union Version: The Underrated Foundation of Modern Vernacular Grammar

The *Mandarin Union Version of the Bible*, published in April 1919, was finished in 29 years, voted through book by book and word by word, by American and British missionaries from seven countries and a number of Chinese literi.

Literary history usually credits the vernacular-literature movement to Hu Shih's *A Modest Suggestion for Reforming Literature* and Lu Xun's *A Madman's Diary*. But as early as 1921, in *The Holy Book and Chinese Literature*, Zhou Zuoren said it point-blank:

> "The Gospel of Matthew is truly the earliest Europeanized vernacular literature in Chinese… In the past two or three years the advocates of the literary revolution have done enough with destruction; after that there should be construction. How should the instrument of this new literature — the vernacular — be organized? I have lately found this remedy in the translation of the Holy Book."

By virtue of its astonishing distribution and the grass-roots evangelism that reached all the way down into the villages, the Union Version established a nationwide consensus of vernacular accent. It was not a trial in a literatus's study; it was a **vernacular grammar standard for all the people**, hammered over a thousand times and comprehensible even to an illiterate farm woman.

### 4. The Soviet-Origin Marxist-Leninist Register: The Fixed Casting of the Prose of National Organized Mobilization

In the 1920s, the translation of Marxist-Leninist writings represented by Qu Qiubai and Chen Duxiu opened up a discourse system with a strong Russian syntactic order and the combatant color of politics. Russian subordinate clauses are complex, its gerunds are rich, and its logical parallelism is tight, and its translation into Chinese spawned a highly organized norm of discourse.

In 1942, during the Yan'an Rectification, Mao Zedong delivered the famous *Opposing Party Dogma* and concentrated his criticism on "strings of empty words," "putting on airs," and "laying out a medicine cabinet labeled 甲乙丙丁 (A, B, C, D)." Ironically, this prose — evolved from the fusion of Soviet-origin political documents with the long sentences of the May Fourth era — did not disappear after the criticism; rather, because of the maturation of national regime-building and the administrative system, it was fully welded into the party press, official documents, and public education.

### 5. The Internet and AI Era: The First Reversal in the Direction of Loanwords in Two Thousand Years

In the 21st century, the internet broke the unidirectional-inflow pattern. More importantly, in the 2020s Chinese reached a historic turning point that had never come before: **Chinese concepts began to flow in the reverse direction into the core contexts of the West**.

"内卷" (*neijuan*, "involuted / involution") — originally the cold, academic English word *involution* used by the anthropologists Goldenweiser (1937) and Geertz (1963) — was creatively given a contemporary metaphor of "over-competition and diminishing returns" in Chinese society, and then, in pinyin form **Neijuan**, surged back into Western media (both the *New Yorker* and *NPR* opened up dedicated in-depth features), and was even brought into *Qiushi* as a key concept for macro-industrial adjustment. The native Chinese word "躺平" (*tǎngpíng*, "lying flat") likewise struck a strong resonance across the world's youth.

Yet behind this outflow, today's general-purpose large models (LLMs) are feeding back, in algorithmic form, the historically sedimented Europeanized passive voice and party-dogma rhetoric to every modern person who taps a keyboard, with unprecedented density.

## II. From the *Analerta* to the Union Version: Quantifying the Refutation of the Cliché that "Translation Brought Europeanization"

To pin down the real transformation that translation wrought on Chinese grammar, we first ran a full word-segmentation and function-word-density measurement on the **classical baseline (the complete *Analerta*, 16,000 characters)**, the **1919 Mandarin Union Version of the *Gospel of Matthew* (1,071 verses / 52,000 characters)**, and the **contemporary standard vernacular translation (CSBT)**.

The data yielded three conclusions that directly overturn the popular view:

### Core Style-Quantitative Data Comparison Table (frequency per thousand characters)

| Core metric | *Analerta* (classical baseline) | Mandarin Union Version (1919 vernacular foundation) | CSBT contemporary translation | Verdict on evolution |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **"之" (zhi)** | **59.77** | **0.82** | 1.09 | **Cliff-zeroing** (−98.6%) |
| **"也" (ye)** | 29.11 | 4.93 | 4.69 | 83% drop |
| **"而" (er)** | 18.35 | 0.25 | 2.34 | 98% drop |
| **矣 / 焉 / 哉 / 兮** | **17.20** | **0.00** | 0.00 | **Zeroed out completely** |
| **於 / 則** | **16.50** | **0.00** | 0.00 | **Zeroed out completely** |
| **"的" (de)** | 1.06 | **45.91** | **47.82** | **Built in one stroke** |
| **"们" (men)** | 0.00 | **27.69** | 33.45 | Plural marker established |
| **"把" (ba) dispositional sentences** | 0.00 | 3.72 | 5.03 | Modern-epoch generative syntax |
| **"被" (bei) passive sentences** | **0.06** | **1.68** | **3.90** | **Gradual doubling over a century** |
| **Average clause length (characters)** | **4.60** | **7.24** | **7.81** | Sentences 69% longer |
| **Share of clauses ≥ 11 characters** | **2.0%** | **16.7%** | **23.0%** | Clear long-sentence trend |

### Conclusion 1: What the Union Version Did Was Not Addition, but a Clean, Decisive "Subtraction from Classical Chinese"

Literary circles often say that "translation introduced a mass of Europeanized long sentences and Westernized structures." But the field data proves that the Union Version's greatest contribution to modern Chinese was **zeroing out, in one stroke, the function-word system of classical *wenyan***:

- "之" (*zhi*), the very core of classical structure, reached 59.78 per thousand characters in the *Analerta* but plummeted to 0.82 in the Union Version;
- "矣 / 焉 / 哉 / 兮 / 於 / 則" (*yǐ / yān / zāi / xī / yú / zé*) — the core markers that maintain the line-breaks and tone of *wenyan* — all became a pure **0** in the Union Version;
- At the same time, the Union Version established in one go the mainstem system of the vernacular: "的 / 了 / 们 / 就" (*de / le / men / jiù*). The density of "的" was, in 1919, raised in a single stroke to 45.91 per thousand characters — nearly indistinguishable from the contemporary translation a century later (47.82).

This was no half-baked, compromise "Europeanization" between East and West; it was a thorough linguistic track-switching project.

### Conclusion 2: The Real Europeanization Was Not Brought by the Union Version, but Grew Wildly After It

Many people blame "translationese" on the May Fourth movement or on Republican-era Bible translation. The data offers the opposite evidence:

- Classical Chinese had almost no formalized passive marker (the *Analerta* has only 0.06 per thousand characters; *wenyan* mostly relied on an implied passive or the "见 / 为…所" *jiàn / wèi…suǒ* pattern);
- The "被" (*bei*) passive in the 1919 Union Version was only 1.68 per thousand characters;
- By the contemporary translation, "被" sentences **surged to 3.90 per thousand characters, more than a 1.3-fold increase**; and "把" (*ba*) dispositional sentences climbed from 3.72 to 5.03.

In other words, **the over-abuse of passive and dispositional sentences is a result that the vernacular has self-reproduced in later literary translation and in bureaucratic prose**. The 1919 Union Version was not bloated at all; on the contrary, it retained with high fidelity the brevity and sharpness of Chinese short-sentence narration.

### Conclusion 3: The Union Version Is a Short-Sentence Vernacular Closer to the Breathing Rhythm of Chinese

In clause length, the *Analerta* averages only 4.60 characters per clause, and long clauses over 11 characters account for just 2.0%; the Union Version averages 7.24 characters per clause, with long clauses at 16.7%; and in the contemporary translation long clauses have already climbed to 23.0%.

**The contemporary vernacular is rapidly "subordinatizing" and "vining," whereas the Union Version in fact represents that crisp, direct, verb-led, bone-framed beauty of the early vernacular.**

## III. Soviet-Origin Marxism-Leninism and the Party-Paper Field Test: The Millennium of Unbroken Four-Syllable Parallelism, and the Carnival of Transitive Verbs

If the Bible established the vernacular framework for everyday narration, then the national political discourse from the Yan'an Rectification through the founding of New China shaped the spiritual face of today's official documents, news, and public-domain expression.

We collected **Mao Zedong's early writings (1927–1948, 110 documents / 539,000 characters)**, **founding-era works and editorials (1949–1957, 67 documents / 283,000 characters)**, and **contemporary core official documents (2018–2024, including the report of the 20th Party Congress, the 2024 government work report, and others, 56,000 characters)**, to build a chain of the evolution of the political register spanning nearly a century.

### Three-Layer Political-Discourse Field-Test Comparison Table (frequency per thousand characters)

| Metric | Early *Selected Works* of Mao (1927–48) | Founding-era documents (1949–57) | Contemporary party press and reports (2018–24) | Evolution trait |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **Four-character idiom density** | **188.85** | **185.45** | **192.79** | **Extreme steady-state (unchanged over a century)** |
| **Three-item parallelism** | 1.05 | 0.54 | 0.27 | Density down, but formalization deepened |
| **"把" (ba) sentences** | 0.90 | 1.13 | 1.20 | Steady use of the dispositional |
| **"被" (bei) sentences** | 0.94 | 0.70 | **0.02** | **Strictly avoided by official news norms** |
| **"的" (de) density** | 42.51 | 37.40 | 15.03 | Shift from essayist polemic to programmatic slogans |
| **Average clause length (characters)** | 37.19 | 31.17 | 37.70 | Long compound-sentence structure stable |

### Core Finding 1: Four-Syllable Parallelism Is the Underlying Operating System of Chinese That Has Never Broken Down Under Any Ideology

This is the most striking set of data in the entire study:

Whether it is the early *Selected Works* of Mao, written during the guerrilla period (188.85), the founding-era policy documents (185.45), or the official political report of the new era (192.79), **the density of the four-character idiom in the full text is locked in at almost 185–193 per thousand characters!**

It shows: **the "four-syllable symmetry gene" that has flowed down from the pre-Qin *Book of Songs*, through the Han–Wei paired-parallel rhapsodies and the Buddhist verses, has never been interrupted by any Westernization or modern revolution.** The reason the official register carries an undeniably solemn, rhythmic feel is that, in its bone, it has inherited the four-syllable cadence of *wenyan*.

### Core Finding 2: The Passive Sentence Has Been Completely Banished from the Official Style

In contemporary official polemic, the density of "被" (*bei*) sentences has plummeted to a stunning **0.02 per thousand characters**.

This is not a weakening of Soviet Russian influence, but **the conscious norm of the modern news style and of political semantics**. Chinese news-writing theory, from Li Yintao's *News Style* in the 1950s, has explicitly stated: "Use the passive less, the active more; the prose must have combat effectiveness and initiative." The subject must be "the Party, the government, the people, the broad cadres"; the subject–object relation must be clear; and the passive sentence, with its passive and "being-controlled" color, has been thoroughly removed from the political register.

### Core Finding 3: The True Legacy of the Soviet-Origin Register Is the "Carnival of Pseudo-Transitive Verbs"

The deepest mark that the Soviet origin left on Chinese is not the passive sentence, but **the flood of transitive-verb-ization of the predicate verb**:

Classical Chinese loved the direct subject–predicate or paratactic structure ("山高月小，水落石出" — "the mountain is high, the moon is small; the water has dropped, the stone emerges"); whereas the Soviet-origin polemic created an entire set of grand transitive-verb chains that *must* take an object:

> **Carry out** the work, **advance** the reform, **implement** the line, **put into effect** the measures, **strengthen** the guarantee, **deepen** the governance, **elevate** the level…

Taken individually these verbs often lack any concrete physical action; their core function is to manufacture a sense of political and administrative authority of "being in a high-intensity state of pushing forward."

## IV. A Full Anatomy of AI Style: 40 Documents Field-Tested Across Five Models, Refuting "Innate Europeanization of LLMs"

Today, every writer complains that what AI spits out has "a certain smell to it": it is either heavy translationese, or an empty reporting style of the system. Exactly where have large models carried modern Chinese to?

To get away from sentimental complaining, we organized two rounds of cross-model field testing:

- **Round 1 (cross-model test)**: For 8 rigorous prompts spanning commercial analysis, science popularization, book reviews, character profiles, and editorials, we had **Monk, Agnes, Gemini, GLM, and DeepSeek (sf-ds)** — the five major mainstream models — each write 8 documents in independent sessions, for a total of 40 documents collected (28,117 characters), and ran a unified quantification script;
- **Round 2 (genre-controlled variable experiment)**: We fixed 2 core propositions and the 500-character requirement, and varied only the genre instruction in the prompt (no instruction / textbook dogma / consulting-firm report / news feature), comparing 16 outputs from GLM and Monk.

### 1. Cross-Model 40-Document Field Test: The Astonishing Range of Grammatical Markers

| Model | "的" density | "把" sentences | "被" sentences | "把+被" total | Four-char idiom | Three-item parallel | Avg. clause length |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **GLM** | 41.29 | **0.00** | **0.00** | **0.00** | **189.26** | 1.53 | 30.75 |
| **Monk** | 24.67 | 3.90 | 2.97 | **6.87** | 163.05 | 2.04 | 29.21 |
| **Agnes** | 32.88 | 3.52 | 1.96 | 5.48 | 167.51 | 1.57 | 32.42 |
| **Gemini** | 25.29 | 3.24 | 2.76 | 6.00 | 171.69 | 1.78 | 30.89 |
| **DeepSeek** | 28.47 | 4.18 | 3.86 | **8.04** | 165.51 | 3.54 | 31.24 |

This table delivers a finding that shatters common sense:

**The Europeanized passive markers like "把 / 被" (ba / bei) are not at all a common, innate attribute of large models!**

Under the same batch of tasks, the combined "把 + 被" total in DeepSeek and Monk reached 7–8 per thousand characters (more than six times the 1.22 of the contemporary party press); whereas Zhipu's domestic **GLM, across all 8 field tests, had the "把 / 被" markers at an absolute 0.00 every time!** At the same time, GLM's four-character idiom density (189.26) was the highest in the whole set, with an average clause length of 30.75 characters.

Why this ice-and-fire, polarized split? After reading the samples, the answer surfaces:

- **GLM is, in its bone, "Chinese textbook / academic-paper style"**: it naturally tends to use hierarchical formatting ("一、1. 2. 3."), makes heavy use of nominalized subjects ("market competition is becoming increasingly fierce, and policy subsidies are gradually being phased out"), and takes the active voice as its mainstay, so it naturally avoids the "把 / 被" sentences;
- **Monk and DeepSeek are, in their bone, "MNC / consulting / translation style"**: they are naturally full of things like "a certain brand has already been left half a generation behind by competitors" and "concentrate resources to break through the user's mind," and they lean heavily on Westernized dispositional and passive constructions.

### 2. Controlled-Variable Experiment: The Genre Instruction Can Push "把 / 被" Up 40 Times!

In the second, strictly controlled round, the results are even more astonishing:

| Genre-instruction tier | GLM "把+被" total | Monk "把+被" total | How to read the gap |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **0. No genre instruction** | 0.00 | 6.38 | Baseline divergence first appears |
| **1. Textbook-dogma style** | 0.00 | 1.26 | Monk also strongly suppressed |
| **2. Consulting-firm report style** | 0.00 | **51.88** | **Monk undergoes extreme pathological amplification** |
| **3. News-feature style** | 0.00 | 10.89 | Returns to narrative normal |
| **Range within genre** | **0.00** | **50.62** | **A single model swings 40×** |

Look at Monk's actual text under the "consulting-report style" instruction:

> "The impact of the night economy on local residents' consumption **has been** verified by multiple urban data sets… residents' per-capita consumption expenditure **has been** structurally raised… local residents **have been** brought into the consumption chain… the increment **has been** released in concentrated form… foot-traffic **has been** spread out across the whole day, and per-square-meter revenue and attach rate **have been** lifted in tandem…"

In just 459 characters, **a frenzied 26 instances of "被" (bei)** were crammed in!

This proves an important mechanism:

**The so-called "AI translationese" is not that the model cannot write Chinese; it is that the large model is extremely sensitive to the genre implication in a particular prompt. The model's training alignment has polished it into an extraordinarily exaggerated prism — it takes the Westernized passive-voice disease inside the consulting style and amplifies it 40-fold before presenting it to you!**

At the same time, GLM displays another kind of tenacious underlying inertia: even when explicitly asked to "use the passive more," it still, throughout the text, uses "elevated the consumption experience, stimulated residents' needs," **resisting the passive instruction with verb complements**. This reveals the ultimate formula of stylistic evolution:

$$\text{Output style} = f(\text{Model's underlying register baseline}, \text{Prompt genre instruction})$$

## V. A "Detox Guide" for Modern Chinese Writers

When we have sorted out the five inflow waves of two thousand years, and clearly see the refinement of classical *wenyan*, the vernacular breakthrough of the Union Version, the four-syllable symmetry of the polemic register, and the pathological amplification of AI toward specific genres — how should today's writer actually put pen to paper?

If you do not want your writing to be reduced to "machine-generated industrial wastewater," nor to "empty prose full of stock phrases," carry with you these **three great detox rules** drawn from the field data:

### 1. Sever the "的" (de) Vine and Eliminate False Nominalization

This is the most lethal legacy of post-May Fourth translationese in modern writing. Indo-European languages such as English like to freeze an action into an abstract noun (e.g., *The consideration of the problem led to the destruction of the plan*); whereas the life of Chinese has always resided in **the living verb**.

- ❌ Vicious Europeanization: "We must **with regard to** the progress of this project **carry out a solid and effective pushing-forward**."
- ❌ Machine tone: "This is **an extremely important indicator's manifestation** aimed at the weakness of market growth."
- ✅ Clean Chinese: "We must **grab time and catch up on the schedule**." / "This indicator shows that market growth is weak."

**Rule: Every time you see "carry out + a two-syllable verb," "the resolution with regard to…," "in the process of…," delete the function words immediately and speak directly with the verb.**

### 2. Expel the Unconscious Pseudo-Passive and Recover Chinese's Topic-Priority

Classical Chinese is a topic-priority language (Topic–Comment): even with no subject, the sentence still stands ("寻寻觅觅，冷冷清清，凄凄惨惨戚戚" — "seeking, seeking; cold and clear; mournful, mournful, desolate, woe-stricken"). Only when meeting misfortune, punishment, or an irresistible force does traditional Chinese rarely bring out "被" (*bei*) (as in "被盗" [was robbed], "被害" [was killed]).

The modern person, contaminated by the English and American passive voice, has instead manufactured a host of laughable passives:

- ❌ Machine tone: "The meeting **was** convened," "a consensus **was** reached," "the data **were** observed by us."
- ✅ Clean Chinese: "We had the meeting," "we reached a consensus," "we saw the data."

**Rule: Unless you are suffering harm, use the active voice where you can, never the passive. Lock "被" out of your daily input method first.**

### 3. Strip Off Hollow Transitive Verbs and Cheap Parallelism; Recover the Concrete Action of the Physical World

The after-residue that the Soviet origin and party-dogma have left us is the habit of stringing parallelism with a set of universal verbs to manufacture a false sense of busyness and sublimity.

- ❌ Bureaucratic stock prose: "We must **effectively elevate** our political standing, **solidly advance** institutional innovation, **thoroughly implement** the service philosophy, and **comprehensively strengthen** team building."
- The fatal flaw in this kind of sentence: in the real world you cannot take a photograph of "advance" or "implement." They are all hollow words drained dry of action detail.
- ✅ Clean expression: Say plainly who is to do what. "The sales rep sees clients three times a week; R&D must compress response time to within 200 milliseconds by month-end."

**Rule: Use fewer four-in-a-row verbs, and write fewer three-item parallelisms that add no information at all. With concrete physical actions and measures of scale, shatter the rhetorical bubble of bluff.**

## Conclusion: The Erosion Goes On; the Corpus Is the Only Thing Not Carried Away

Over two thousand years, every great loanword shock to Chinese has, in the end, settled into the muscle memory that today's writers use without thinking:

- The causal universe of Sanskrit has turned into the Zen flavor within Tang–Song poetry;
- The modern skeleton of Japanese-made Chinese has propped up contemporary social-science thinking;
- The vernacular function words of the Union Version let a farmer with little schooling understand the same language;
- The iron discipline of the Soviet-origin polemic has been received, but the underlying rhythm of four-syllable symmetry has not been washed away.

Today AI mixes the historically sedimented Europeanized passive, bureaucratic stock phrases, and concrete action, and pours them into everyone's input box at millisecond speed. What the writer can do is not much: check the origin of every piece of grammar, delete the surplus "的" (*de*), lock out the contrived "被" (*bei*), and swap the hollow verbs for concrete actions. The corpus is still in the vault; the numbers can be recomputed. As to what the next wave of erosion will bring, this article does not predict — it simply lays open the ledger from the past two thousand years up to the present.
